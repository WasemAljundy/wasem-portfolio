# Recruiter Conversion

## Purpose
Design the information architecture so a busy recruiter can identify seniority, fit, credibility, and contact options within seconds, while deeper technical detail remains available for engineering reviewers.

## Trigger
Use for homepage hierarchy, navigation, hero copy, calls to action, project ordering, resume placement, contact flows, and portfolio audits.

## Visitor Questions
The site should answer quickly: Who is he? What role is he targeting? What technologies and product areas does he own? What proof exists? Is he available/relevant? Where is the CV? How do I contact him?

## Hierarchy Rules
- Lead with role and value, not autobiography.
- Put strongest proof early.
- Show 3-5 selected projects before exhaustive history.
- Make CV view/download visible but not the only proof.
- Make contact friction low.
- Keep primary CTA stable and clear.
- Avoid forcing a recruiter through animations before content.

## Dual-Audience Design
Recruiter layer: concise role, expertise, selected wins, status, CV, contact. Engineering layer: expandable case studies with architecture, trade-offs, testing, performance, and technical decisions.

## Credibility Signals
Use real project statuses, live links, GitHub links where appropriate, certifications only when valid, clear dates, truthful role labels, and professional contact channels. Avoid fake testimonials, fake logos, artificial counters, skill percentages, and self-assigned 100% ratings.

## Conversion Review
Audit time-to-understanding, CTA clarity, information scent, mobile contact flow, CV discoverability, project scanability, dead ends, and whether decorative effects compete with proof.

## Definition of Done
A visitor can understand positioning and reach CV/contact without hunting, while technical reviewers can drill into evidence without cluttering the first scan.
